II. The Tide Rises, the Tide Falls by H. W. Longfellow [8 Marks]

"The morning breaks; the steeds in their stalls
Stamp and neigh, as the hostler calls;
The day returns, but nevermore
Returns the traveller to the shore,
And the tide rises, the tide falls."

a) What signs of morning activity are described in this stanza? What do the steeds and hostler represent?

[2]

b) Identify the poetic device in: "The little waves, with their soft, white hands / Efface the footprints in the sands". Explain its deeper significance.

[2]

c) What contrast does the poet draw between the transient existence of mortal human life and the eternal rhythm of nature?

[2]

d) Why does the poet conclude each stanza with the refrain: "And the tide rises, the tide falls"?

IV. Comparative Character Study [7 Marks]

Answer either (a) OR (b) in about 80–100 words:

(a) Compare and characterize Halku and Munni in Premchand's January Night. Contrast Munni’s practical outrage against the landlord's continuous extortion with Halku’s submissive resignation and dread of public humiliation. How do their contrasting attitudes illustrate the helplessness and tragedy of tenant farmers?

— OR —

(b) Compare and characterize the "ancient sister" and the mother in Roald Dahl's A Drive in the Motor Car. Contrast the sister's reckless overconfidence and panic behind the wheel with the mother’s calm authority and presence of mind when dealing with the accident and the author's injury.
